# Open questions

An open question is closed by a citation, a counterexample, a proof, or a
benchmark artifact. "It seems reasonable" does not close one.

| OQ-1 | Is solver-guided adaptive read selection already published, in phasing or in a neighbouring assembly problem? | end W3 | open, and no deliverable depends on it. Every search it listed was run on 25 Sep; section G of the prior-art matrix has the results. The ingredients are all published: the margin is a min-marginal gap (Kohli and Torr 2008), steering data toward low-margin decisions is margin sampling (Settles 2010), and BOSS-RUNS (Weilguny et al. 2023) chooses reads by genotype uncertainty during sequencing. Margin-guided reselection among reads already sequenced, under a coverage cap, was not found. It stays open because a search that finds nothing does not close a question under the rule above, and the report states component 3 as margin sampling applied to this problem. Narrowed 24 Sep: the margin itself is not new, since ProbHap (Kuleshov 2014) computes a per-boundary confidence from forward and backward DP tables and HapCUT2 reports switch quality, but none of the sources in section F of the prior-art matrix feeds such a confidence back into read selection. Narrowed 10 Sep. WhatsHap 2015 and HapCHAT 2018 are read in full and neither does it: WhatsHap computes no phase confidence from its DP tables and has no backward pass, and HapCHAT adapts a per-column error bound rather than a selection. Removing the two nearest candidates is not an answer. Still unsearched: the LP-sensitivity and active-learning literatures on their own terms, the polyploid and metagenomic assembly literatures, and the WhatsHap issue tracker where a deployed heuristic can change without a paper. Section D of the prior-art matrix records the search and its gaps |
| OQ-2 | Does the published Fischer and Marschall selection optimize the additive objective solved here, or a different one? | end W3 | closed 10 Sep, and measured 24 Sep: it reaches 0.870 to 0.926 of the certified additive optimum in `benchmarks/benchmark_heuristic_gap.py`. It is a coverage and connectivity heuristic, not the additive score of `select_optimal`. The implementation and source notes are in `docs/design/published-selection-objectives.md`. | OQ-5 | Do MEC margins predict switch errors, and does the signal decay as the objective stops tracking the truth? | end W10 | answered on simulation 26 Sep, and this is the project's central hypothesis. Pooled over the milestone-1 grid of depth, error, density and base-quality spread, 130 cells, the log objective-truth gap correlates with informativeness at -0.893 for both the MEC margin and the posterior margin; informativeness is 0.95 to 1.0 below a gap of 1 and falls to 0.6 near 10. | OQ-6 | Is the accuracy gain from adaptive selection stable, or an artefact of the iteration cap and the boost factor? | end W14 | closed 10 Sep, stable. `benchmarks/benchmark_sensitivity.py` over 4 boosts, 3 quantiles, and 3 iteration caps beats static selection in 213 of 216 cells, worst case +0.0029 switch error. Marginal effects are monotone and mild: boost barely matters between 1.25 and 4.0, lower quantiles are better, more iterations are better with no sign of oscillation. The gain is not a parameter artefact. The separate limit, recorded in the development notebook, is that only 2.5 to 5.4 of 12 instances per cell improve at all Carried to chromosome scale 26 Sep with stitched acceptance: every setting of window (10 to 200), iterations (2 to 8) and boost (1.25 to 4) beats static selection in every cell, and a held-out run confirmed window 25 |
| OQ-7 | Does the complementary-haplotype restriction cost accuracy on real data, where a site called heterozygous can be a genotyping error? | end W12 | closed 26 Sep at scale. `phasebudget.general` is an exact general-model dynamic program checked against the bipartition oracle, and `benchmarks/benchmark_genotype_errors.py` now runs at 60 variants and about 100 reads. The restriction trades completeness for accuracy at every miscall rate: the general model declines 9 to 21 per cent of sites and is 3 to 10 times more accurate on the rest. It costs bipartition accuracy only from a miscall rate of about 0.10 at error 0.02, and 0.30 at error 0.10. Below 0.05 the complementary model is at least as good on every column, which covers GIAB-grade genotypes; a caller's own genotypes need re-measuring. Numbers in the development notebook |

| OQ-10 | Does the margin signal survive when the DP is run on a selected subset rather than on all reads? | end W12 | resolved 10 Sep in the regime where it can be tested, and untestable where it matters. `benchmarks/benchmark_margin_confound.py` compares subset margins against a full-matrix oracle over 60 replicates. Subset margins are a genuinely different vector, rank correlation 0.26 to 0.56 with negative worst cases, but they are not a worse steering signal: at a matched budget of one reweight each the two sources split three cells to three. Boundaries the subset hides from the loop are 0.7 to 2.0 per cent of spanned boundaries. So the gain does not depend on privileged access to the subset's thinness, since an oracle with true fragility information does no better. The limit is structural: the oracle needs the whole matrix solved, so every instance tested is one selection was not needed for, and the confound stays untested at the coverages selection exists for |

| OQ-12 | Which HG002 truth file scores the real-data run? | end W18 | closed 26 Sep, by `benchmarks/build_trio_truth.py`. The primary truth is phasing by transmission from the parents' v4.2.1 calls, `docs/design/truth-sets.md`, which no read-based phaser touched: on chromosome 20 it phases 36,860 of 46,344 heterozygous SNVs, 79.5 per cent; 17.4 per cent are ambiguous because both parents are heterozygous. | OQ-13 | Does the adaptive loop's gain come from its fragility signal or from reselecting and rescoring? | end W15 | answered 26 Sep: from reselecting and rescoring. A control that moves the posterior's fragile boundaries to random places keeps almost all of the gain at 60 variants and at chromosome scale; the signal beats the control significantly in 2 of 9 small-scale cells, loses in 1, and is never significant at scale. Stitched acceptance, `docs/design/adaptive-acceptance.md`, is what makes the loop work at chromosome scale. The development notebook has the tables. On HG002 HiFi reads (M5) the loop gave no gain: at caps 5 and above it stopped after one round, and at cap 3 it raised switch errors from 2 to 17, identically with the shuffled control, through a stitching defect (OQ-15). On 27 Sep two-level phasing (`docs/design/two-level-phasing.md`) narrowed the mechanism: orienting the selected subset's blocks exactly by every read leaves switch error unchanged on simulation, while the loop lowers it in 6 of 7 held-out cells with errors, so the loop's gain comes from changing haplotypes inside blocks through reselection, not from how blocks are joined |
| OQ-14 | Does the selector WhatsHap ships do what Fischer and Marschall 2016 describe? | end W15 | answered 26 Sep: no. `select_whatshap`, a port of WhatsHap 2.8's `readselect.pyx`, selects the same reads as WhatsHap on 1,500 of 1,500 small and 36 of 36 chromosome-scale instance and cap pairs; the paper's procedure matches on 178 of 1,500. The main departure is the score update, which lowers a queued read by its variants the admitted read did not newly cover instead of those it did. At chromosome scale and cap 15 the shipped code reaches 0.915 and 0.916 of the certified optimum, slightly more than the paper's procedure; at cap 3 it leaves 22 to 27 phase blocks where the paper's rule leaves 1 or 2. `docs/design/published-selection-objectives.md` and the development notebook have the details. The score update has not been reported to the WhatsHap authors |
| OQ-15 | Does stitched acceptance, which orients only at window junctions, cost accuracy when the selected reads break into blocks inside windows? | end W22 | closed 27 Sep. Yes on HG002 at cap 3 (all 15 extra switch errors on junctions, each after a block break of the round's selection inside the window). Repaired by `acceptance="stitched_oriented"`, which reorients every segment between a junction or a support break jointly and exactly by every read: on HG002 chromosome 20 (post hoc, masked truth) the loop's cap-3 switch errors fall from 15 to 0 at full depth and from 10 to 2 at half depth, equal to static selection, and on held-out simulation the loop's switch error is unchanged to within 0.00002. The genome-wide registration tests the repair as G6 |
| OQ-16 | How should a trio spend WhatsHap's coverage budget, whose pedigree DP grows with the total number of active reads across members? | end W22 | answered on simulation 27 Sep (`benchmarks/benchmark_trio_budget.py`). One exact selection over the union of the three members' reads under a single cap (`shared`) at a total of 9 matches WhatsHap's `C // 3` split at 15 on the child's switch error for ONT-like and noisy reads, with a pedigree DP about 60 times faster (1.3 to 1.7 s against 85 to 99 s); WhatsHap's split at 9 does not on noisy reads. The registered arm weighted alleles at transmission-ambiguous sites and was worse, so the rule as registered is not met and the unweighted result is post hoc. The registered real-data trio run was scored on 1 October (the development notebook) |
