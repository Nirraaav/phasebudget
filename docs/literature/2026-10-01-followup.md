## 1. The even-count identity P(2k) = P(2k-1)

### Ben-Yashar 2023

- Crossref: Ruth Ben-Yashar, "An application of simple majority rule to a
  group with an even number of voters", *Theory and Decision* 94(1):83-95,
  online 23 March 2022, issue January 2023, DOI 10.1007/s11238-022-09872-1.
- Open full text: none found. Unpaywall and OpenAlex both report
  `oa_status: closed` with no repository copy. The Bar-Ilan CRIS page and the
  RePEc/IDEAS page carry the abstract only. Springer's page returns a bot
  challenge.
- Abstract (RePEc, verbatim): "In the basic model of Condorcet's jury theorem
  and in the literature that follows, an odd-numbered group of voters is
  assumed so that the simple majority rule can be used. We show that this
  assumption is not necessary either in Condorcet's basic model or in the
  general framework of dichotomous choice. We first apply simple majority
  rule to an even-numbered homogeneous fixed-size committee. We then provide
  a justification for using simple majority rule for an even-numbered
  heterogeneous fixed-size committee when the competence structure of the
  committee members is not common knowledge."
- Finding: whether the paper states P(2k) = P(2k-1) under a fair coin cannot
  be determined from what is accessible. The abstract does not mention
  tie-breaking. Its reference list (Crossref) includes Berend and Sapir 2005
  and Feld and Grofman 1984, both read below.
- Status: `abstract`. The present sentence in `paper.tex` ("we have read only
  the abstract") is accurate; the paper should not cite Ben-Yashar for the
  identity.

### Primary sources that state the identity

**Berend and Sapir 2005** (the classic statement found).
Crossref: Daniel Berend, Luba Sapir, "Monotonicity in Condorcet jury
theorem", *Social Choice and Welfare* 24(1):83-92, February 2005, DOI
10.1007/s00355-003-0293-z. Read from the author's copy at
`tzin.bgu.ac.il/~lsapir/Papers/CJT.pdf`, a 14-page preprint whose footer
reads "3.tex; 10/08/2003". Page numbers below are the preprint's; the journal
version was not seen.

- Setting: a group of n voters with competences p_1, ..., p_n, independent;
  a random subcommittee of size m decides by simple majority. For even m,
  p. 4: "It is natural, though, to extend the rule to this case by tossing a
  coin if the votes are split evenly within the committee." The probability
  of a correct decision for even m is defined with half the weight of the
  tied configurations.
- Theorem 1, p. 5, for competences with p_i >= 1/2 for each i:
  M̄_m <= M̄_{m+1} for m even, 0 <= m <= n-1, and M̄_m = M̄_{m+1} for m odd,
  1 <= m <= n-1; "Moreover, for any even m we have M̄_m = M̄_{m+1} if and only
  if p_i = 1/2 for 1 <= i <= n." It is followed by the chain
  "M̄_0 <= M̄_1 = M̄_2 <= M̄_3 = M̄_4 <= ...".
- Introduction, p. 3: "if m is odd, then increasing it by 1 leaves the
  probability exactly the same." The coin rule is attributed there to
  Berg 1993.
- The equality is proved as equation (4), p. 8: M̄_{2k+1} = M̄_{2k+2}.
- Bearing: for a homogeneous group (all p_i = p) every subcommittee of size
  m has the same probability, so M̄_m is the probability for a committee of
  size m, and Theorem 1 gives P(2k) = P(2k-1) for p >= 1/2. The paper's
  Proposition (wrong-reading probability q in (0, 1/2)) is the case p = 1 - q
  > 1/2, so it is covered.
- Status: `paper` (author's preprint, full text; the introduction, the
  definitions, Theorem 1 and the statement of equation (4) were read; the
  lemmas and the rest of the proof were not checked).

**Andersson and Angner 2026** (a recent, open, self-contained statement).
Crossref: Tommy Andersson, Erik Angner, "A 250-Year-Old Theorem Walks into a
Courtroom", *The Mathematical Intelligencer* 48(3):270-273, online 6 August
2026, DOI 10.1007/s00283-026-10556-9, CC BY 4.0. It is a column in "The
Factotum" series, not a research article. Full text PDF read.

- Theorem 1, p. 271, assumes n odd, independent voters, and "the same
  independent probability p in (0, 1) of voting correctly".
- Remark 1, p. 272, verbatim: "To avoid technicalities concerning ties,
  Theorem 1 is stated for odd jury sizes. If n = 2m is even and ties are
  broken by an independent fair coin, let P_2m(p) denote the probability of
  a correct verdict. Couple a jury of size 2m with one of size 2m - 1 by
  letting the two juries share the first 2m - 1 voters and adding one extra
  voter to the even jury. [...] These two effects exactly cancel in
  expectation in the sense that the gain in the second case matches the
  loss in the first, and hence P_2m(p) = P_2m-1(p). Thus, restricting
  attention to odd n entails no loss of substance: with fair tie-breaking,
  the full sequence satisfies P_2m(p) = P_2m-1(p), while the strict
  comparisons occur when moving from 2m to 2m + 1."
- The Remark's argument is the same coupling as the paper's proof of
  Proposition `prop:parity`. It gives no citation for the identity.
- Status: `paper` (full text; Theorem 1 and Remark 1 read).

### Recommendation

Cite Berend and Sapir 2005 for the identity, with Andersson and Angner 2026
as an open statement of it for the homogeneous case. Keep Ben-Yashar 2023
only as related work on even committees, or drop it. A replacement for the
sentence in `paper.tex`: "The identity is Theorem 1 of Berend and Sapir
\cite{berend2005}, stated there for random subcommittees of voters with
competence at least 1/2; Andersson and Angner give the coupling argument for
the homogeneous case \cite{andersson2026}."


**Mallona et al. 2025.** Izaskun Mallona, Charlotte Soneson, Ben Carrillo,

- Section "Building trust and bringing together communities": "there are
  many field experts who may wish to conduct a benchmark (or participate in

  41 and 42 are Sullivan et al. 2019 (Open Science Framework) and Olevska et

- Both quotes in the sweep file are exact. One wording issue in

  pre-registration as recent work to mitigate the conflict and then notes a
  tension with continuous benchmarking. A closer sentence: "Mallona et al.
  describe pre-registration as a way to mitigate the conflict when
  developers benchmark their own tools \cite{mallona2025}."
