\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Motivation:} Read-based phasers decide which heterozygous
variants share a haplotype from a subset of the reads, and report the
decision with no error rate. We ask two questions: how much the choice of
reads matters, and whether a phase relation can carry an error rate that
holds on genomes it was not fitted to.

\noindent\textbf{Results:} \emph{Read selection.} WhatsHap selects reads down
to a coverage cap before its exact dynamic program. Selection is solved
exactly by min-cost flow. At the default cap of 15, WhatsHap 2.8's selector
reaches 0.933 of the optimum on HG002 HiFi, 0.963 on HG002 ONT and 0.934 on
HG005 HiFi. In a registered test inside \texttt{whatshap phase}, exact
selection lowered ONT switch error at cap 3 by 42 per cent
($p=8.4\times10^{-28}$) and shortened blocks; at caps 5 and 15 no difference
was detected. Most of the larger differences in our own library were
guesses at tied sites. \emph{Certified relations.} A post-processor marks
each relation between consecutive phased variants as trusted or not, by the
Wilson lower bound on agreeing reads, with a threshold chosen by Learn then
Test within GIAB difficult-region strata so that the probability that a
100\,kb unit holds a wrong trusted relation is at most $\beta$ with
confidence $1-\delta$. In six registered tests on 48 individuals, one
genome-wide threshold failed on a new genome; per-stratum thresholds held in
the easy stratum in all 582 testable cells, across relatives, basecallers and
39 HPRC genomes of three ancestries calibrated on one European genome, while
trusting more than 99.5 per cent of easy pairs. In the hard strata,
calibration on one genome violated in 2 of 1{,}170 testable cells, but a
calibration pooled over 21 genomes, which made segmental duplications
certify, violated in 163 of 360 cells on a new family's HiFi reads. In
development the cause was the calibration truth: it labelled a link only
where an assembly and parental transmission agreed, which kept 46 to 49 per
cent of duplication and tandem-repeat links against 75 per cent of easy
ones; on the links a key of the same kind labelled in the new family, the
pooled thresholds violated in no cell, against 120 cells when the same share
of links was kept at random. A registered replication on 19 held-out HPRC
genomes against their assemblies alone found the same: no violating cell on
key-labelled links, 1{,}221 of 2{,}052 cells on all links, and hard-region
links wrong 14 to 103 times as often overall as on key links; so did a
registered test on NA12878's HiFi reads, never compared with a truth
before (31 of 108 cells violating on all links, none on links her own
family key labels). Trusting
a relation only when a single-sample assembly of the same person agrees with
it, a sixth registered test pooled six genomes and held in all 62 testable
cells on two new genomes, certifying segmental duplications at
$\beta=10^{-3}$ with 0.78 and 0.96 of their pairs trusted; with the same
certificates, a registered test certified cis or trans calls for 0.60 to
0.89 of within-gene variant pairs at a per-call error of $10^{-2}$; in
development, checking the assembly only at a call's two end variants raised
the certified share of pairs 10 to 100\,kb apart on the father from 0.55 to
0.88 and 0.90, and a registered test on NA12878 certified 0.89 and 0.90 of
them. Over 37 certificate tests in this project, every violation
was on pairs outside the population the calibration labels came from, or on
a new sequencing batch, and no test with matched labels violated. We prove
that certifying a new sequencing batch needs about $\ln(1/\delta)/\beta$
labelled units of that batch (about 3{,}000 at $\beta=10^{-3}$). In
development, parental genotype calls supply them for the pairs they can
label, and a registered replication on HG005 with labels from its
parents' calls held in all 90 testable cells; scored against the complete
Q100 assembly, relations outside the
v5.0q benchmark region hold 53 and 94 per cent of two phasers' errors
(on NA12878, against a transmission truth, 85 and 91 per cent outside GIAB's
region, registered), and
where parental genotype calls reach the pairs on which phaser and assembly
disagree, they attribute 0.64 to 0.94 of those disagreements to the phaser;
and
read-derived flags make the tandem-repeat stratum certifiable.

\noindent\textbf{Availability:} \url{https://github.com/Nirraaav/phasebudget}:
code, registrations with their commit times, run files and scorers. All
data are public.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Read-based phasing assigns the alleles of heterozygous variants to the two
haplotypes of a diploid genome from reads that cover several variants. The
usual objective is minimum error correction (MEC), the fewest allele calls to
change so that the reads split into two consistent groups~\cite{lippert2002};
its weighted form wMEC charges each change by the confidence of the
call~\cite{greenberg2004}. MEC is NP-hard~\cite{lippert2002}. WhatsHap solves
wMEC exactly with a dynamic program exponential in coverage~\cite{patterson2015}
and therefore first selects reads down to a cap with a greedy
heuristic~\cite{martin2016,fischer2016}; Ralphi reuses that
selector~\cite{ralphi2025}. The result is written into the \texttt{GT} and
\texttt{PS} fields of a VCF. The VCF specification reserves a \texttt{PQ}
field for the probability that alleles are ordered incorrectly and leaves the
measure undefined;\footnote{VCF v4.3, Section 1.6.2,
\url{https://samtools.github.io/hts-specs/VCFv4.3.pdf}.}
WhatsHap~\cite{whatshap2015,whatshap2016}, HiPhase~\cite{hiphase} and
LongPhase~\cite{longphase} do not fill it.

Two questions follow, and this paper answers both. The first concerns the
input to the phaser: how far is the deployed selector from the best
selection under the same cap, and what does the gap cost in switch error?
Choosing the heaviest reads under a per-variant cap is an interval scheduling
problem, exactly solvable by min-cost flow~\cite{arkin1987,carlisle1995}, so
exact selection can serve as the reference (Section~\ref{sec:selection}).

The second concerns the output: can a relation between two phased variants
carry an error rate? The question matters most for in-trans calls: under the
ACMG/AMP guidelines a variant in trans with a pathogenic variant in a
recessive disorder is moderate evidence (PM3)~\cite{richards2015}, and
laboratories rely on familial genotyping or read-based phasing to establish
it~\cite{delgobbo2025}. Statistical phasing reports a per-genotype phase
probability~\cite{shapeit5}, chosen against a cutoff
empirically~\cite{lassen2024}; a read-based relation has no comparable
number. Ranking relations is not the hard part: a score from read support
separates wrong relations from right ones with an AUC of 0.998 on HG002 HiFi
(Section~\ref{sec:score}). What is missing is a threshold whose error rate is
stated and holds on data it was not fitted to. We treat this as selective
classification with guaranteed risk~\cite{geifman2017,ltt2025}: trust a
relation or abstain, so that a stated risk among trusted relations is at most
a target. Errors are dependent along the genome, so the risk is stated over
100\,kb units; and the error rate at a given score differs between regions,
so thresholds are calibrated within the GIAB difficult-region
strata~\cite{dwarshuis2024}, which is Vovk's conditional
construction~\cite{vovk2012} applied to Learn then Test.

Every confirmatory claim below comes from a test registered before its test
data were read: a design document committed and pushed to a public
repository, with hypotheses, levels and decision rules; deviations are dated
amendments. Results marked \emph{post hoc} were found after the data were
seen; results marked \emph{development} come from a gate written before it
was computed, on data that may have been seen before.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Exact read selection by min-cost flow, a port that reproduces
  WhatsHap 2.8's shipped selector read for read, and the selector's distance
  from the optimum on HiFi and ONT reads of HG002 and HiFi reads of HG005
  (Section~\ref{sec:selection}).
\item Registered tests of where selection changes accuracy, in our library
  and inside \texttt{whatshap phase}: exact selection lowers ONT switch error
  by 42 per cent at cap 3 and changes nothing detectable at caps 5 and 15;
  post hoc, most of the library's larger differences were guesses at tied
  sites; and a majority-vote model whose registered regime rule failed.
\item A post-processor that needs only a BAM and the \texttt{GT} and
  \texttt{PS} fields of any phaser's VCF, with a per-stratum threshold and a
  finite-sample guarantee at the level of genome units
  (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
\item Five registered tests of that guarantee on 48 individuals
  (Section~\ref{sec:results}), including its failures.
\item A lower bound on the labelled units a new sequencing batch needs
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:floor}), and three development results: labels from
  parental genotype calls (Section~\ref{sec:labels}), the errors outside the
  benchmark region (Section~\ref{sec:outside}), and read-derived flags for
  tandem repeats (Section~\ref{sec:flags}).
\item A mechanism for the one failure of pooled calibration: a truth that
  labels a relation only where two methods agree selects easy relations, and
  thresholds fitted on it hold only on relations like those it labels
  (Section~\ref{sec:t5cause}), found in development and replicated in a
  registered test on 19 held-out genomes; and an account of all 37 certificate tests in
  the project by that rule (Section~\ref{sec:principle}).
\item A within-gene cis or trans call that checks the assembly at the two
  variants reported, which certifies most long-range pairs at a per-call
  error of $10^{-2}$ (Section~\ref{sec:endcalls}), with five predictions
  for a new genome (NA12878) registered before its phase existed, all
  supported (Section~\ref{sec:na12878}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Exact phasing and read selection.} Exact MEC solvers differ in
the parameter they pay for: coverage in WhatsHap~\cite{patterson2015}, read
length~\cite{he2010}, corrections per column~\cite{pirola2016,beretta2018},
or an integer program per block~\cite{chen2013}; MEC is fixed-parameter
tractable in coverage for fixed ploidy~\cite{bonizzoni2016}. Heuristic
solvers have no coverage parameter~\cite{edge2017,duitama2012,das2015}.
Fischer and Marschall wrote that they were comparing their selector with the
flow algorithm of M\"akinen et al.~\cite{fischer2016,makinen2015}; in the
citing works we read~\cite{martin2016,beretta2018,garg2018} we found no
report of that comparison, and the split-interval version of selection is
APX-hard~\cite{bareyehuda2006}. Martin et al.\ compared the deployed selector
with random downsampling~\cite{martin2016}. HiPhase~\cite{holt2024} and
LongPhase~\cite{lin2022} phase from all reads, so the question does not arise
for them. Information-theoretic analyses give the coverage needed to recover
a whole haplotype~\cite{kamath2015,chen2016,mahajan2024}; our vote model is
local and finite. Teo et al.\ published a benchmark as a Registered
Report~\cite{teo2024}, and Mallona et al.\ describe pre-registration as a
check on developers benchmarking their own tools~\cite{mallona2025}, which is
the situation here.

\paragraph{Phase confidence.} HapCUT2~\cite{hapcut2} prunes variants whose
largest configuration posterior is below 0.8 and splits blocks at low
posteriors; we did not find a measurement of whether these posteriors are
calibrated. ProbHap~\cite{probhap} computes per-SNP transition
probabilities by forward-backward. PhaseQuality~\cite{damaraju2026} assigns
variant pairs to confidence tiers, which are not probabilities.
PhaseME~\cite{phaseme} checks a phased VCF against population linkage.
HipHap~\cite{hiphap} reports a per-read haplotype assignment quality against
a diploid assembly. Paraphase~\cite{chen2025paraphase} phases paralogous
genes with a dedicated caller. Clinical studies report concordance on small
sets of variant pairs~\cite{jamshidi2025} or no phase error
rate~\cite{devaney2026}.

\paragraph{Risk control.} Geifman and El-Yaniv~\cite{geifman2017} define
selective risk and choose a threshold with a high-probability bound.
Conformal risk control~\cite{crc2024} needs a loss monotone in the
threshold, which selective risk is not. Learn then Test~\cite{ltt2025}
casts risk control as multiple testing with finite-sample guarantees for
any loss; our procedure is its selective classification example with genome
units in place of points. Vovk~\cite{vovk2012} calibrates within categories
fixed in advance; Gibbs et al.~\cite{gibbs2023} handle overlapping groups.
Under covariate shift, calibration can be reweighted with a known
likelihood ratio~\cite{tibshirani2019}; a shift of the error rate at a given
score within a stratum, which is what a basecaller change can cause, is not
covered.

\paragraph{Truth sets and families.} GIAB v5.0q~\cite{hansen2026} is built
from the complete HG002 Q100 diploid assembly; its authors report that the
earlier v4.2.1 benchmark left out 12 per cent of the autosomes; the Platinum Pedigree~\cite{kronenberg2025} filters
variants by inheritance in a three-generation family. Genotype errors bias
trio-based estimates of phase accuracy~\cite{browning2022}. Relatives
sharing segments by descent have served as surrogate parents for
phasing~\cite{kong2008} and siblings as phasing truth for statistical
phasing~\cite{oconnell2016}.

\section{Certified phase relations: method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Pairs, units and risk}\label{sec:problem}

Fix a chromosome and its heterozygous variants in position order. A phaser
assigns each variant $j$ either nothing or a first-haplotype allele
$h_j\in\{0,1\}$ and a block $b_j$. A \emph{pair} $p=(j,j')$ is two variants
consecutive among the phased ones and in one block, with called relation
$\rho_p=\mathbf{1}[h_j=h_{j'}]$. A pair is \emph{compared} when the truth
phases both variants, with relation $\rho^*_p$, and \emph{wrong} when
$\rho_p\neq\rho^*_p$.

A score $s(p)$, which never reads the truth, takes values in a totally
ordered set. At threshold $t$ the trusted set is $T(t)=\{p:s(p)\ge t\}$. The
genome is cut into units $u$ of 100\,kb, and a pair belongs to the unit of
its first variant. Let
\[
a_u(t)=\mathbf{1}[\exists p\in u\cap T(t)],\qquad
\ell_u(t)=\mathbf{1}[\exists p\in u\cap T(t),\ p\text{ wrong}].
\]
A unit is \emph{active} when it holds a trusted pair and \emph{wrong} when it
holds a wrong trusted pair. For units drawn from a distribution $D$ the
\emph{risk} is $R(t)=\Pr_{u\sim D}[\ell_u(t)=1\mid a_u(t)=1]$. One flipped
site breaks both of its pairs, so a per-pair loss would count one error
twice; the unit loss counts it once. A relation between two variants of one
unit is wrong only if the unit holds a wrong pair on the chain between them,
so the unit loss also bounds an in-trans call inside one unit. Both $a_u$
and $\ell_u$ are non-increasing in $t$, but $R$ is not monotone.

\paragraph{Goal.} From calibration units choose $\hat t$ with
$\Pr[R(\hat t)\le\beta]\ge1-\delta$, preferring rules that trust more pairs.

\subsection{The score}\label{sec:score}

For a pair, let $a_p$ be the number of reads that call both variants and
agree with $\rho_p$, $d_p$ the number that disagree, and $m_p=a_p+d_p$. The
score is $s(p)=(L(a_p,m_p),m_p)$ ordered lexicographically, where $L$ is the
Wilson lower bound with $z=2$ on the agreeing share $\hat q=a_p/m_p$,
\[
L(a,m)=\frac{\hat q+\frac{z^2}{2m}-z\sqrt{\frac{\hat q(1-\hat q)}{m}+
\frac{z^2}{4m^2}}}{1+\frac{z^2}{m}},\qquad L=-1\text{ when }m=0.
\]
It penalizes a pair supported by few reads, which the agreeing share does
not, and a pair with many reads and many disagreements, which the support
margin $a_p-d_p$ does not. Reads are extracted as \texttt{whatshap phase}
extracts them (realignment against the reference, mapping quality at least
20, no supplementary alignments), from all reads, not from a downsampled
subset. On development data (HG002, 21 chromosomes, HiFi) its AUC for
separating wrong from right pairs was 0.998, against 0.995 for the margin.

\subsection{Choosing the threshold}\label{sec:ltt}

The grid $t_1\succ t_2\succ\cdots\succ t_G$ holds the score values at which
50, 50.25, \dots, 100 per cent of calibration pairs are trusted ($G=201$),
ordered from strict to loose. For $t_g$, let $N_g$ be the number of active
calibration units and $K_g$ the number of wrong ones; the p-value of
$H_g:R(t_g)>\beta$ is $\pi_g=\Pr[\Bin(N_g,\beta)\le K_g]$. The scan rejects
$H_1,H_2,\dots$ while $N_g>0$ and $\pi_g\le\delta$, and outputs the last
rejected threshold, or nothing if $H_1$ is not rejected.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:guarantee}
Assume (A1) the calibration units are independent draws from $D$, (A2) the
grid and its order are fixed before the calibration losses are observed,
and (A3) the truth is correct on the compared pairs. Then
$\Pr[\hat t\text{ is output and }R(\hat t)>\beta]\le\delta$.
\end{proposition}

This is the fixed-sequence form of Learn then Test~\cite{ltt2025}; the
proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}. The grid is computed from
calibration scores, which do not use the truth but come from the same
units, so (A2) holds approximately; computing it from separate chromosomes
removes the gap. (A1) is an assumption: on development ONT data, wrong
10\,kb bins clustered ($p=0.0005$) while wrong 100\,kb units did not (within
one unit, 1 adjacent pair against 1.14 expected, $p=0.69$), which chose the
unit size.

\paragraph{Testing on new data.} Given $\hat t$, a test set yields $n$ active
units and $k$ wrong ones. If the test units are independent draws with risk
at most $\beta$, then $\Pr[\Bin(n,\beta)\ge k]$ is a valid p-value for
exceeding $\beta$. Every validity hypothesis below uses it, with a
Bonferroni level over its cells.

\subsection{Calibration within strata}\label{sec:strata}

A pair is \texttt{indel} if either variant is an indel; else
\texttt{segdup}, \texttt{lowmap} or \texttt{tandem} if either variant lies
in that GIAB v3.6 stratum~\cite{dwarshuis2024}, in that order; else
\texttt{easy} if both lie in the stratum outside all difficult regions;
else \texttt{difficult}. The unit becomes (chromosome, 100\,kb bin, group),
and the scan runs within each group on its own rows and grid.
Proposition~\ref{prop:guarantee} then holds per group, each at confidence
$1-\delta$. A group with no certified threshold trusts nothing. A per-group
statement does not depend on how a test genome's pairs are spread over the
groups, so it needs no reweighting between genomes with different
difficult-region content.

\subsection{Relations within a block}\label{sec:chains}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:chain}
Let a relation between two variants of one block be trusted when every pair
between them is trusted, let its pairs lie in $m$ distinct (group, unit)
cells of $q$ groups, and let each group's threshold be certified at
$\beta$. If test units are exchangeable with calibration units within each
group, then with probability at least $1-q\delta$ over the calibration, the
relation is trusted and wrong with probability at most $m\beta$.
\end{proposition}

The bound grows with the number of units a relation spans, and a single
untrusted pair blocks the relation; Section~\ref{sec:discussion} reports
what this means for variant pairs within genes.

\section{How many labels a certificate needs}\label{sec:theory}

The scan certifies only if $\pi_1\le\delta$, and
$\pi_1\ge\Pr[\Bin(N_1,\beta)=0]=(1-\beta)^{N_1}$. Hence:

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:units}
A group certifies $\beta$ only if it has at least
$N^*(\beta,\delta)=\lceil\ln\delta/\ln(1-\beta)\rceil$ active calibration
units. At $\delta=0.05$, $N^*$ is 998, 2{,}995 and 9{,}985 for $\beta=3\times
10^{-3}$, $10^{-3}$ and $3\times10^{-4}$.
\end{corollary}

The segmental-duplication group of one genome holds about 2{,}900 units at
30-fold (2{,}868 and 2{,}924 on HG002 ONT for the two phasers), so in one
genome it cannot certify $10^{-3}$ whatever the score: the smallest
certifiable $\beta$ is $1-0.05^{1/N}$, $1.04\times10^{-3}$ and
$1.02\times10^{-3}$. The next result shows that the requirement is not an
artefact of the procedure.

\begin{proposition}[Label floor]\label{prop:floor}
Let a procedure see the losses $\ell_1,\dots,\ell_n$ of $n$ labelled units of
a batch, independent Bernoulli with mean $R$, and any other data whose
distribution given the losses does not depend on $R$. If it certifies with
probability at most $\delta$ whenever $R>\beta$, and with probability at
least $p>\delta$ when $R=0$, then $(1-\beta)^np\le\delta$, that is,
$n\ge\ln(p/\delta)/(-\ln(1-\beta))$.
\end{proposition}

At $p=1$, $\beta=10^{-3}$ and $\delta=0.05$ the bound is 2{,}994.2 units, which
Learn then Test with no wrong unit attains. Units from other batches help
only through an assumption that ties their error rate to the new batch's;
without it they are part of the ``other data'' and cannot lower $n$.
Prediction-powered and pooled corrections are covered by the same argument.
In practice a new batch, such as a new basecaller release, needs labels at
the scale of a genome.

\begin{proposition}[Selected populations]\label{prop:select}
Let a rule $E$, fixed in advance and not reading the truth, mark pairs
eligible. Learn then Test run on the units formed by eligible calibration
pairs controls $R_E$, the risk over units formed by eligible pairs, at
$\beta$ with confidence $1-\delta$ under (A1) to (A3) for that distribution.
It says nothing about the risk over all pairs.
\end{proposition}

Proofs are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}. Proposition~\ref{prop:select} is
used twice: for labels that exist only for some pairs
(Section~\ref{sec:labels}), and for abstention on pairs that a read-derived
flag marks (Section~\ref{sec:flags}). When labels come from relatives, (A3)
becomes the assumption that the labels are right on eligible pairs.

\section{Certified phase relations: registered experiments}\label{sec:design}

\paragraph{Registration.} Each test was a design document committed and
pushed to a public repository before its test reads were drawn; the push
time is the third-party record. It fixed the score, unit, grid, $\delta=0.05$,
$\beta\in\{3\times10^{-4},10^{-3},3\times10^{-3}\}$, depths, phasers,
hypotheses, Bonferroni levels and decision rules. Deviations are dated
amendments stating whether results had been seen; none below was made after
a test result was seen. Each test cell calibrates at its own depth with its
own phaser, and the test population is consecutive pairs of variants the
test truth phases, with the truth's own heterozygous calls, phase removed,
as phaser input. In T4 the input check, registered to run before the
scorer, ran while the scorer ran; it reads no phase, and every test-genome
row it found had been written by the scorer. Phasers were \texttt{whatshap phase} 2.8 with
\texttt{--reference} and \texttt{--ignore-read-groups} at its default
\texttt{--internal-downsampling} of 15, and LongPhase 2.0.2 (\texttt{--ont}
or \texttt{--pb}, with \texttt{--indels} from the second test on), at 30, 20
and 10-fold slices drawn with fixed seeds, on the 21 autosomes other than
chromosome 20, which served as a pilot. Table~\ref{tab:design} lists the

\paragraph{Truth quality decides what can be tested.} A test truth with
error rate $e$ per unit raises a cell's observed rate by up to $e$. For the
HPRC genomes, the registered truth was dipcall on each genome's trio
assemblies. Before any test genome was scored, it was compared with GIAB
trio transmission on HG002 and HG005: in the \texttt{easy} group 278 of
47{,}737 units disagreed (one-sided 95 per cent bound $6.4\times10^{-3}$), so
a single-assembly truth cannot test a unit bound of $3\times10^{-3}$.
Amendment 1 of T4 replaced it, before any test genome's phasing was
compared with a truth, by the consensus of dipcall and transmission from
the 1000 Genomes high-coverage panel's trio genotypes. A consensus pair is
wrong only where the transmission pair is wrong, so transmission's own
error, measured on NA12878 against the Platinum Pedigree truth, bounds the
consensus truth's. A (group, $\beta$) is testable when that bound's one-sided
95 per cent upper limit is at most $\beta/3$ (Table~\ref{tab:gate}). Only
39 of 44 candidate HPRC genomes have both parents in the 1000 Genomes
pedigree; the other five are not scored.

\section{Certified phase relations: results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{One threshold does not transfer between genomes (T1)}

With one threshold for the whole genome, calibrated on HG002 ONT
super-accuracy reads, no cell of HG002 high-accuracy reads violated the
bound, and all 18 HG001 cells did, with realized unit rates 3.6 to 42 times

\subsection{Why pooled calibration failed (development)}\label{sec:t5cause}

The registration of T5 named one risk (units of different genomes at one
locus are not independent), and two others were plausible: the consensus
truth labels only pairs where dipcall and transmission agree, which may
keep easy pairs (Proposition~\ref{prop:select}), and the Pedigree HiFi reads
are a different sequencing batch. We tested all three after T5 was scored,
with rules written before computing (\nolinkurl{docs/design/l1-key-selection.md}).

\paragraph{The key selects easy pairs.} On HG002's HPRC HiFi reads, scored
against v5.0q, we compared pairs the consensus-style key (HG002's HPRC
dipcall equal to GIAB trio transmission) labels with pairs it does not. The
key labels 0.75 of \texttt{easy} pairs but 0.46 of \texttt{segdup}, 0.48 to
0.49 of \texttt{tandem} and 0.66 of \texttt{lowmap} pairs, and no indel pair.
At 30-fold, over \texttt{segdup}, \texttt{lowmap} and \texttt{tandem}
together, all pairs were wrong 79 times as often as key-labelled pairs for
WhatsHap ($4.7\times10^{-3}$ against $5.9\times10^{-5}$; pairs the key does
not label $9.9\times10^{-3}$) and 11.6 times for LongPhase ($1.4\times10^{-3}$
against $1.2\times10^{-4}$; unlabelled $2.8\times10^{-3}$); the rule was a
ratio above 3 for both. The smallest single-group ratio was 3.7
(LongPhase, \texttt{lowmap}); in the \texttt{difficult} group LongPhase's
ratio was 0.89.

\paragraph{The failure disappears on key-labelled pairs.} For each of the
five Pedigree children we built a key of the same kind: the child's
single-sample hifiasm assembly relates the pair with the relation that
transmission from the 1000 Genomes genotypes of NA12877 and NA12878 gives.
Rescoring T5's 540 cells with its pooled thresholds reproduced the 163
violating cells on all pairs. On key-labelled pairs no cell violated. As a
control for lost power, keeping pairs at random with the same share per
cell left 120 violating cells (rule: at most 32 on key pairs and at least
82 at random). The failure is selection, not fewer units.

\paragraph{Batch and shared loci.} On key-labelled pairs the children were
not more error-prone than the 20 HPRC pool genomes (WhatsHap \texttt{segdup}
$2.3\times10^{-4}$ against $7.6\times10^{-4}$; LongPhase
$1.5\times10^{-4}$ against $4.3\times10^{-4}$), so a batch effect does not
explain T5. Wrong units are shared at a locus: of a child's wrong WhatsHap
\texttt{segdup} units, 0.85 were also wrong in a sibling and 0.32 in an HPRC
pool genome.

\paragraph{Replication on 19 held-out genomes (registered).} The results
above explain a failure already seen, in one family. We repeated the
comparison on the 19 held-out HPRC genomes of T5, with rules committed
after a development run on the 20 pool genomes and before any held-out
genome was scored this way (commit \texttt{0d984c1},
\nolinkurl{docs/design/l8-hprc-key-selection.md}). The truth was each
genome's HPRC dipcall alone, and the key was the consensus truth of T5;
links where dipcall and transmission both phase but disagree (0.038 to
0.084 per cent of links) were left out. With T5's pooled thresholds, over
$19\times108=2{,}052$ cells at level $0.05/2{,}052$, all three rules held.
At 30-fold the wrong rate in \texttt{segdup}, \texttt{lowmap} and
\texttt{tandem} together was more than 3 times higher on all links than on
key links in 19 of 19 genomes for both phasers (smallest ratios 14.3 for
WhatsHap and 31.9 for LongPhase; the rule needed 17 genomes). On all links
1{,}221 cells violated, and every genome had a violating hard or indel cell
(the rule needed 15). On key links no cell violated. Links kept at random
at the key's share left 1{,}159 violating cells. On links that transmission
does not label, dipcall is the only truth, so its own errors count as
phaser errors there. A second registered test, on NA12878, uses a truth
from transmission alone (Section~\ref{sec:na12878}).

\subsection{Requiring an assembly to agree (T6)}\label{sec:t6}

In development on HG002 HiFi, a hifiasm assembly of HG002's HiFi reads alone,
called by dipcall, was right on all 478 hard-stratum pairs that WhatsHap got
wrong and it phased (Appendix~\ref{app:other}). T6 makes agreement a
condition of trust. The assembly (hifiasm 0.25.0, default options, no
parental or Hi-C data; dipcall 0.3) \emph{relates} two variants when each
lies in one pair of haplotype contigs, the same for both, inside dipcall's
region, with a phased call at the same alleles; a pair \emph{agrees} when
the assembly relates it with the phaser's relation, and a pair that does not
agree is never trusted. Calibration pooled the agreeing pairs of HG002 and
the five Pedigree genomes of T5 at 30-fold, a unit being (genome,
chromosome, 100\,kb), so that \texttt{segdup} has more units than the floor
of Section~\ref{sec:theory} requires. The test genomes were NA12877, the
Pedigree father, whose reads the project had not read, and HG005's HPRC HiFi
reads, never assembled before; 72 cells, level $0.05/72$. The registration
stated the shift: NA12877 shares a haplotype with each pool genome, so HG005,
unrelated and sequenced elsewhere, is the test of transfer. Before scoring,
a dry run with HG002's even chromosomes as a stand-in test certified
\texttt{segdup} at $10^{-3}$ and violated in no agreement cell.

All three registered hypotheses were supported. No cell violated: 62 of the
72 cells had a trusted unit, and the smallest $p$ was 0.818 (NA12877,
WhatsHap, \texttt{segdup}, $10^{-3}$: 2 wrong units of 3{,}119). With
18{,}667 (WhatsHap) and 18{,}736 (LongPhase) pooled agreeing \texttt{segdup}
units, \texttt{segdup} certified at $10^{-3}$ for both phasers, though not at
$3\times10^{-4}$. Table~\ref{tab:t6} gives the trusted shares at $10^{-3}$.
The same pool without the agreement rule also had no violating cell (50
testable, smallest $p$ 0.793) but certified nothing in \texttt{segdup} for
either phaser and nothing in WhatsHap's \texttt{indel} group. Agreement does
not make errors independent: on NA12877 every wrong pair among agreeing
pairs was also wrong in the assembly (WhatsHap: 10 \texttt{indel}, 6
\texttt{segdup}, 1 \texttt{tandem}), against 6{,}175, 1{,}147 and 357 wrong
pairs among the pairs that did not agree. It removes most errors because
the assembler and the phaser fail in different places.

\subsection{Checking only the two variants reported (development)}\label{sec:endcalls}

A chain call is wrong only if the phaser's parity between the two end
variants is wrong, yet the chain rule above needs every link between them
to agree with the assembly, so one link the assembly does not call blocks
the call. We replaced it with an end rule: a within-gene call is trusted
when the assembly relates the two end variants with the phaser's relation.
Calibration used the same pool, one hash-chosen pair per (pool genome,
gene, distance bin, easy or hard), with a group certified at per-call
target $r$ when $\Pr[X\le k\mid n,r]\le0.05$; rules were written before
computing (\nolinkurl{docs/design/l2-end-variant-calls.md}), on the test
genomes of the registered test above, so the results are development.

At $r=10^{-2}$ the certified share of NA12877's pairs 10 to 100\,kb apart
was 0.896 (WhatsHap) and 0.884 (LongPhase), against 0.553 and 0.599 with
the chain rule; on HG005 0.941 and 0.938 against 0.862 and 0.870. Over all
of NA12877's 30.2 million within-gene pairs the share was 0.896 and 0.884.
No cell violated (0 to 2 wrong picks of about 2{,}200 per cell, level
$6.25\times10^{-3}$). Requiring both phasers and the assembly to agree at the
ends kept the share (NA12877 0.883, HG005 0.946) and cut wrong certified
pairs on NA12877 from 498 (WhatsHap) and 2{,}081 (LongPhase) to 88; 82 per
cent of NA12877's 2{,}672 genes and 97 per cent of HG005's 2{,}498 had a
certified pair, with median per-gene shares 0.93 and 1.0. Neither rule
certified any pair 10 to 100\,kb apart at $r=10^{-3}$: the pool's 11{,}500
picks per group allow at most 5 wrong, and the three-way rule had 6. The
duplicated genes stay out of reach (on NA12877 with WhatsHap, OTOA 0 of
2{,}145 pairs, CYP11B2 0 of 1{,}431, PMS2 78 of 2{,}346). Reads spanning
both ends, used instead of the assembly, certified only 0.07 to 0.15 of
pairs beyond 10\,kb.

\section{Labels from the sequencing batch}\label{sec:labels}

Thresholds calibrated on one sequencing batch did not always hold on
another: in development, pooling six ONT genomes and holding each out in
turn, every one of 23 violating cells (of 1{,}512) had HG002's 2025 reads held
out, so the pool was mostly older ONT data. Proposition~\ref{prop:floor}
says what a fix must pay. On HG002's 2025 reads as the new batch and six
other genomes as the pool (development), Learn then Test on $k$ sampled
HG002 units certified no group at $\beta=10^{-3}$ for $k=300$, 1{,}000 or
3{,}000 (20 seeds each), as the bound requires, since a group's units are
fewer than $k$; with all about 11{,}800 easy units of HG002's odd chromosomes
it certified \texttt{easy} and \texttt{difficult} in 4 of 4 cells, with no
violating cell for any $k$. The pool alone violated, and merging the pool
with HG002's units violated until HG002's units dominated.

A clinical sample has no benchmark truth, but it may have parents sequenced
in the same batch. A site of the child where one parent is homozygous is
phased by transmission, and a pair of such sites is a label. With labels
from Clair3 calls on the parents' ONT reads (HG003 and HG004, two flowcell
replicates each), requiring both replicates to agree brought the label error
in the easy group to $1.33\times10^{-5}$ per pair, below WhatsHap's own
$1.84\times10^{-5}$ there, with 0.776 of pairs labelled. Calibrating on
labelled pairs and testing on all pairs, one cell violated
(\texttt{lowmap} at $3\times10^{-3}$, 190 wrong units of 43{,}599), because
labelled pairs are easier: WhatsHap's error on unlabelled \texttt{lowmap}
pairs was $3.82\times10^{-3}$ against $6.96\times10^{-5}$ on labelled ones.
Proposition~\ref{prop:select} prescribes the repair: test on eligible pairs
only and abstain on the rest. Then no cell violated in 108 (level
$1.54\times10^{-4}$); the cell that failed had 42 wrong units of 38{,}087
($1.10\times10^{-3}$); \texttt{easy} at $10^{-3}$ certified in 6 of 6 depth and
phaser cells, and 0.774 of all easy pairs were trusted. \texttt{segdup},
\texttt{tandem} at $10^{-3}$ and \texttt{indel} did not certify. Pairs
touching a site where the two replicates' parental calls disagreed had
WhatsHap error $4.4\times10^{-2}$ in \texttt{lowmap} against $7.0\times10^{-5}$
for eligible pairs (observed after the run). These are development results
on one trio of a reference sample.

\paragraph{A registered replication.} The same procedure was registered for
a second trio and batch (commit \texttt{a147ef3}, 27 October 2026): HG005 ONT
sup v5.0.0, labels from ONT's \texttt{wf-human-variation} calls on two
flowcells each of HG006 and HG007, the \texttt{agree} filter, thresholds from
eligible odd-chromosome pairs, scored on eligible even-chromosome pairs
against GIAB's trio transmission; 108 cells, level $0.05/108$. Before
scoring, the generalized scorer reproduced the HG002 output above line for
line. No cell violated (90 had pairs; smallest $p$ 0.44), and \texttt{easy}
at $10^{-3}$ certified in 6 of 6 depth and phaser cells with 0.993 of even
\texttt{easy} pairs trusted; both registered hypotheses were supported. At
$10^{-3}$, \texttt{difficult}, \texttt{lowmap} and \texttt{tandem} also
certified in every cell, and \texttt{segdup} only at $3\times10^{-3}$. The
trusted share is not comparable with HG002's 0.774. HG005's truth is trio
transmission, which phases only sites where a parent is homozygous, so 99.2
to 99.6 per cent of the scored pairs were eligible (observed after scoring);
the test shows validity on a second trio and batch, not how much of a
genome the procedure covers.

\section{Errors outside the benchmark region}\label{sec:outside}

Every test above scored relations inside a benchmark region. To see what
lies outside, we phased HG002 HiFi at 30-fold (HPRC reads realigned with
minimap2 2.31) on the heterozygous SNVs and indels of the Q100 dipcall truth
inside its BED (2{,}607\,Mb of the 21 chromosomes, against 2{,}521\,Mb for
v5.0q's benchmark BED), with phase removed, and scored consecutive relations
against Q100. A relation is \emph{inside} when both variants lie in v5.0q's
benchmark BED and \emph{outside} otherwise (development; gate: error per
pair outside at least 3 times inside and at least 20 per cent of wrong

relation at every outside wrong relation, so this comparison cannot
separate truth errors from phasing errors there. These are the regions
v5.0q's authors judged less reliable. One genome, one read type, one depth.
The finding is that a phasing benchmark scored inside v5.0q's region sees
half or less of the errors, not that every outside error is the phaser's.

\section{Flagging read-supported errors}\label{sec:flags}

Most hard-region errors are supported by the reads, so the Wilson score
cannot reject them. We tested flags that a user computes from the sample's
own reads (development): allele balance at each site, as a binomial
$-\log_{10}p$; the excess wMEC cost of the exact solver over a window of two
variants on each side, normalized by reads; read depth against the
chromosome median; and 31-mer copy excess from the reads. On HG002 HiFi,
per group, a subset of flags and a cutoff were tuned on odd chromosomes at a
flagged share of 0.09; flagged pairs were abstained on, and Learn then Test
ran on unflagged pairs (Proposition~\ref{prop:select}). The window cost was
checked against exhaustive enumeration on fixed and random windows.

On even chromosomes, the flags found 0.795 of wrong \texttt{tandem}
relations (0.553 to 0.891 per depth and phaser cell) and 0.988 of the 165
confidently wrong ones (every spanning read wrong, at least 10 reads), with
at most 8.9 per cent of the group flagged. In the three cells where
\texttt{tandem} had certified nothing at $\beta=10^{-3}$, it certified in all
three, and no flagged cell violated (90 cells, level
$5.56\times10^{-4}$). Allele balance and the window cost carried the signal
(single-flag recall in \texttt{tandem} at 30-fold, WhatsHap: 0.786 and 0.810;
depth 0.097, $k$-mers 0.097). With the settings chosen on HG002 ONT and
applied unchanged to HG001 ONT, recall in \texttt{tandem} was 0.767 and the
one \texttt{tandem} cell that had certified nothing certified, without a
violation. The flags also found 0.78 to 0.84 of wrong relations in
\texttt{segdup} and \texttt{lowmap}, but \texttt{segdup} gained no
certificate at $10^{-3}$: it holds about 1{,}650 calibration units on HG002's
odd chromosomes, fewer than $N^*=2{,}995$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:units}).

\section{One rule behind every result}\label{sec:principle}

Proposition~\ref{prop:select} says what a certificate controls: the risk on
the population its calibration labels come from. Table~\ref{tab:principle}
groups all 37 certificate tests of this project by whether the test pairs
were drawn from that population. Every test with a violation drew test
pairs from outside it; every matched test had none. Two failures fall
outside the rule: thresholds pooled from older ONT batches failed on HG002's
2025 reads (23 of 1{,}512 cells, all with that batch held out), so the
rule needs the same sequencing batch as well, and one K1b development run
mixed truths and batches and cannot be assigned. The rule predicts whether
a certificate fails, not by how much: HG002's rates on and off its key
predicted the children's all-pair rates only within a factor of 0.24 to 13.9.
The rule is Learn then Test's exchangeability assumption~\cite{ltt2025},
broken in a way that is easy to miss when a truth set is itself a
selection. It was stated after most of these tests were scored; the
registered tests on NA12878 and on the 19 held-out HPRC genomes (rows L7
and L8) were scored after it and fit it.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Read selection.} At WhatsHap's default cap, the deployed
selector's shortfall from the optimum (4 to 7 per cent of the objective) did
not appear in accuracy inside the tool, on HiFi or ONT, nor in pedigree mode,
whose tests had little power. At cap 3 on ONT, exact selection is more
accurate and less contiguous. The larger differences our library measured
were mostly guesses at tied sites, which a phaser that abstains leaves
unphased; a comparison of selectors should report abstentions beside
errors. The choice of tool changed HiFi switch error more than the choice of
selector (HiPhase 0.052 against 0.165 per 10{,}000 for stock
\texttt{whatshap phase}, each on its own pairs, untested).

\paragraph{Certified relations.} Per-stratum risk control gave read-based
phase relations an error rate that held, in the easy stratum, in all 582
testable cells, on every genome, basecaller and ancestry we tested, while
trusting more than 99.5 per cent of easy pairs. Its limits are as clear as
its success. First, it is only as general as its calibration: thresholds
pooled from older ONT batches failed on a new one in development, a
calibration pooled over 21 HiFi genomes failed in the hard strata of a new
family (T5), because the consensus truth it was calibrated on labels easy
hard-stratum pairs (Section~\ref{sec:t5cause}), and Proposition~\ref{prop:floor} shows that no procedure
certifies a new batch from a few hundred of its own labels. A trio sequenced
on the batch supplies genome-scale labels for the pairs its calls can label.
Second, it abstains in segmental duplications and on WhatsHap's indel
relations with calibration on one genome, and pooling genomes to make them
certify broke the bound, unless a trusted relation must also agree with an
assembly of the same person (T6), which held on two new genomes and trusted
most hard-stratum pairs. That certificate needs an assembly, so it applies
to HiFi data at full depth. Third, a benchmark region sees a small part of the
errors: on HG002, most wrong relations lie outside v5.0q's region, and
thresholds calibrated inside do not hold there. In tandem repeats, flags
computed from the reads recover most read-supported errors.

For in-trans calls, Proposition~\ref{prop:chain} gives a per-relation bound,
but a relation spanning tens of kilobases crosses many pairs and one
untrusted pair blocks it. On HG002 ONT at 30-fold, of 33{,}055{,}724 pairs of
variants within one clinically relevant gene and at most 100\,kb apart, the
chain was certified at a per-call target of $10^{-2}$ for 0.53 of pairs up to
1\,kb apart and 0.003 of pairs 10 to 100\,kb apart (WhatsHap), mostly because
of untrusted indel pairs (development). Requiring
assembly agreement (Section~\ref{sec:t6}) lifted this, in a registered test
on two new genomes, to 0.60 to 0.89 of all within-gene pairs and 0.55 to 0.87
of pairs 10 to 100\,kb apart, without a violating cell, and checking the
assembly only at the two variants reported lifted pairs 10 to 100\,kb apart
to 0.88 to 0.94 (development) and to 0.89 and 0.90 on NA12878 (registered); some duplicated genes such as PMS2 still
abstain, because the assembly does not resolve them.

The practical lesson of Section~\ref{sec:principle} is for anyone who
calibrates against a truth set: calibrate and test on pairs chosen by the
same rule, and report the rule. A truth that exists only where two methods
agree, or only inside a benchmark region, or only where parents are
informative, gives a certificate for that population and no other.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The selection results rest on HG002 for ONT, the
tool-level tests and the trio, with HG005 replicating the HiFi results; only
SNVs inside the v4.2.1 benchmark regions were measured there, and every
subsample used one seed. The certificate assumes independent units, and at
100\,kb they are not independent in the hard groups: over every genome with
a full truth, wrong units of the same (platform, depth, phaser, group)
adjoined more often than under a within-chromosome permutation in 181 of 576
tests at level $0.05/576$, including 78 of 96 \texttt{segdup} and 60 of 96
\texttt{indel} tests. Merging neighbouring units into 200 and 300\,kb units
and refitting the pooled thresholds of T5 and T6 made no decision less
conservative (0 of 360 cells) (development,
\nolinkurl{docs/design/l5-unit-independence.md}). The explanations of Sections~\ref{sec:t5cause}
and~\ref{sec:endcalls} and the parental adjudication of
Section~\ref{sec:outside} are development results on data seen before;
registered tests on NA12878 and on 19 held-out HPRC genomes supported the
first two, and the parental adjudication has no registered test. Test truths have their own errors, which is why T4
and T5 test only some cells. Relatives in T2 share haplotypes with their
calibration genome, and T3's test genome had been studied on other reads.
The development results of Sections~\ref{sec:labels} to~\ref{sec:flags}
come from one or two genomes and need registered tests; of these, only the
batch labels have one so far (HG005), and its truth limits it to validity.

\section{Proofs}\label{app:proofs}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:flow}]
For $j\in\{1,\dots,n\}$ the arcs leaving $S_j=\{v_0,\dots,v_{j-1}\}$ are the
chain arc $(v_{j-1},v_j)$ and the read arcs with $a_r\le j\le b_r$; no arc
enters $S_j$, since every arc goes to a higher index. For a flow $f$ of value
$c$, conservation therefore gives, for every $j$,
$f(v_{j-1},v_j)+\sum_{r:\,a_r\le j\le b_r}f_r=c$. If $f$ is integral and
feasible, $f_r\in\{0,1\}$, and $x_r=f_r$ satisfies $\sum_{r\ni j}x_r\le c$
because the chain flow is non-negative; the cost of $f$ is $-\sum_rw_rx_r$.
Conversely, a feasible $x$ gives the flow $f_r=x_r$,
$f(v_{j-1},v_j)=c-\sum_{r\ni j}x_r\in[0,c]$. At each inner node $v_j$ the
inflow minus the outflow is $\sum_{r\ni j}x_r-\sum_{r\ni j+1}x_r-
\sum_{b_r=j}x_r+\sum_{a_r=j+1}x_r=0$, because a read covers $j$ but not $j+1$
exactly when $b_r=j$, and $j+1$ but not $j$ exactly when $a_r=j+1$; the value
is $c$ by the cut identity at $j=1$; the cost is $-\sum_rw_rx_r$. Both maps
preserve cost, the capacities are integral and a feasible flow exists (all
flow on the chain), so an integral min-cost flow exists and gives an optimal
selection.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:guarantee}]
\emph{Each p-value is valid.} Fix $g$. Under (A1), conditional on $N_g=n$, the
$n$ active units are independent draws from $D$ conditioned on $a_u(t_g)=1$,
so $K_g\sim\Bin(n,R(t_g))$. Under $H_g$, $R(t_g)>\beta$, and the binomial lower
tail decreases in the success probability, so $\Pr[\pi_g\le\delta\mid
N_g=n]\le\delta$ for every $n$, hence also unconditionally.
\emph{The scan controls the family-wise error.} By (A2) the order is fixed and
$R$ is a fixed function, so the index $g^*$ of the first true null is not
random. If $\hat t=t_{\hat g}$ is output with $R(\hat t)>\beta$, then $H_{\hat
g}$ is a true null that was rejected, so $g^*\le\hat g$, and since the scan
rejects in order, $H_{g^*}$ was rejected. That event has probability at most
$\delta$. (A3) makes the observed losses the true ones.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:chain}]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:guarantee} each of the $q$ groups has
$R_g(\hat t_g)\le\beta$ except with probability $\delta$; by the union bound
all hold with probability at least $1-q\delta$. On that event, the relation
is a parity of its pairs' relations, so it is wrong only if some pair on the
chain is wrong; if all pairs are trusted, some cell of the chain then holds
a wrong trusted pair, which for each of the $m$ cells has probability at
most $\beta$. The union bound over cells gives $m\beta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:floor}]
Write $P_R$ for probabilities when the unit risk is $R$, $C$ for the event
that the procedure certifies, and $Z$ for the event that all $n$ losses are
0. Under $P_0$, $Z$ is sure, so $P_0[C\mid Z]=P_0[C]\ge p$. Given the losses,
the other data and any internal randomization have the same distribution
for every $R$, so $P_R[C\mid Z]=P_0[C\mid Z]$. For $R=\beta+\varepsilon$ with
$0<\varepsilon<1-\beta$, validity gives
$\delta\ge P_R[C]\ge P_R[Z]\,P_R[C\mid Z]\ge(1-\beta-\varepsilon)^n p$.
Letting $\varepsilon\to0$ gives $(1-\beta)^np\le\delta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:select}]
The rule is fixed in advance and does not read the truth, so a unit's
eligible pairs, their scores and their losses are functions of that unit's
data. Under (A1) for $D_E$ the calibration units formed by eligible pairs
are independent draws from $D_E$; the grid is computed without the truth as
in Proposition~\ref{prop:guarantee}; under (A3) their losses are the true
losses. Proposition~\ref{prop:guarantee} with $D_E$ in place of $D$ gives the
claim. Ineligible pairs enter neither the calibration nor $R_E$, so the risk
over all pairs is not bounded.
\end{proof}

\section{Other directions tested}\label{app:other}

Each of these had a design note and a gate committed before it was computed
(development, \nolinkurl{docs/design/}); outputs are in
\nolinkurl{benchmarks/results/runs/}.

\paragraph{Parent of origin from methylation.} On HG002 ONT reads, with
imprinted DMRs from~\cite{akbari2022} and methylation per haplotype, the
parent carried along WhatsHap's phase blocks from 131 recovered DMRs was
wrong for 32{,}152 of 198{,}729 variants (16.2 per cent; 3.5 per cent within
100\,kb of the DMR, 28 per cent beyond 1\,Mb). Certified blocks assigned 586
variants, none wrong, but the phaser was also right on all 586.

\paragraph{Learned scores.} Gradient boosting on 22 read features raised
WhatsHap's trusted share in \texttt{tandem} at $\beta=10^{-3}$ (from 0 to
0.861 on HG002 ONT) and certified nothing in \texttt{segdup}; a neural score
did no better there. Pooling six ONT genomes for calibration and holding
each out, all 23 violating cells of 1{,}512 had HG002's 2025 reads held out.

\paragraph{A complete reference.} Realigning HG002 HiFi to CHM13: of 27
matched SNV relations that WhatsHap got confidently wrong on GRCh38 (every
spanning read wrong, at least 10 reads), 25 stayed wrong on CHM13.

\paragraph{Labels from called parental genotypes.} Parental Clair3 calls
from one flowcell gave labels wrong at $2.06\times10^{-4}$ per easy pair,
above WhatsHap's own error there ($1.84\times10^{-5}$), so calibration on
them certified nothing at $\beta\le10^{-3}$; requiring both replicates to
agree fixed this (Section~\ref{sec:labels}).

\paragraph{Truth errors without a gold standard.} A latent-class fit of
four sources of NA12878 phase was not stable between odd and even
chromosomes (1000 Genomes transmission, \texttt{tandem}: $4.4\times10^{-4}$
against $1.5\times10^{-4}$), and no truth reached $10^{-3}$ error in a hard
group. Where all sources phase a relation, the Pedigree and Platinum 2017
truths never disagreed.

\paragraph{Siblings without parents as labels.} Labelling a child from
siblings' genotypes in segments inferred to be shared identical by descent
gave label error $2.73\times10^{-2}$, then $2.82\times10^{-3}$ after a
repair, against $10^{-4}$ required: all remaining wrong labels lay in truly
unshared stretches that hold no opposite homozygote within 250\,kb, which
two siblings' genotypes cannot tell from shared ones.

\paragraph{Clinical in-trans chains.} See Section~\ref{sec:discussion}: 0.037
(WhatsHap) and 0.143 (LongPhase) of within-gene pairs up to 100\,kb apart
were certified at a per-call target of $10^{-2}$ on HG002 HiFi; no sampled
certified call exceeded its bound (60 cells).

\paragraph{Error loci learned on one genome.} Bins holding HG002's HiFi
errors raised the error rate of other people's pairs only 0.5 to 2.1-fold,
against 4.6 to 21-fold for HG002's own ONT reads: error loci belong to the

\paragraph{A single-sample assembly.} hifiasm on HG002's HiFi reads alone,
called by dipcall: against v5.0q it was right on all 478 hard-stratum pairs
that WhatsHap got wrong and it phased (\texttt{segdup}, \texttt{tandem}),
but in \texttt{easy} it had 2{,}131 wrong of 1{,}249{,}172 pairs (switches
inside contigs), and the trio truth phased too few hard pairs to check it.

\paragraph{A calibration-size planner.} The probability that Learn then
Test certifies, computed from a pool's unit error rate, did not predict how
often subsamples of one to six genomes certified: 13 of 51 informative
cells agreed (rule: 0.8), with predictions too high in 23 of the 38 others.
Genomes differ in error rate and a resampled genome adds no new units. The
arithmetic floor stands: \texttt{segdup} at $\beta=10^{-3}$ with K1b's pooled
rate ($1.6\times10^{-4}$) needs about 6{,}300 units, three genomes, for
certification probability 0.8.

\paragraph{HiFi and ONT as each other's second opinion.} On HG002, five
Pedigree members and HG005, of HiFi's wrong \texttt{segdup} pairs that ONT
also relates, ONT made the same error on 0.39 to 0.59 for the Pedigree
members and HG002 (gate: below 0.2; HG005 had 3 and 6 such errors), and agreement cut the error rate only 2
to 5-fold; both platforms are aligned to the same reference and fail
together. Platform agreement does not replace an assembly.
