\section{Exact read selection}\label{sec:selection}

\subsection{The selection problem}\label{sec:selproblem}

WhatsHap solves weighted minimum error correction (wMEC) exactly with a
dynamic program whose state is exponential in coverage~\cite{patterson2015},
so it first selects reads down to a cap $c$ per variant~\cite{martin2016}
with a greedy heuristic~\cite{fischer2016}. A read covers the variants in its
span; an interior gap carries no allele but still occupies coverage. For read
weights $w_r$, the sum of a read's allele log-odds, the additive selection
problem is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selection}
\max \sum_r w_r x_r \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \sum_{r\ni j}x_r\le c\ \
\text{for every variant } j,\qquad x_r\in\{0,1\}.
\end{equation}
Each read occupies a run of consecutive variants, so the constraint matrix
has consecutive ones in each column and the problem is a min-cost flow
(interval scheduling with capacity $c$)~\cite{arkin1987,carlisle1995}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:flow}
Let read $r$ cover the variants $a_r,\dots,b_r$. On nodes $v_0,\dots,v_n$
put a chain arc $(v_{j-1},v_j)$ of capacity $c$ and cost 0 for each $j$, and
a read arc $(v_{a_r-1},v_{b_r})$ of capacity 1 and cost $-w_r$ for each read.
An integral min-cost flow of value $c$ from $v_0$ to $v_n$ exists, and the
reads whose arcs carry flow solve (\ref{eq:selection}), with optimal value
equal to minus the flow's cost.
\end{proposition}

The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}. The optimum bounds how far any
other selection is from it. The implementation matches exhaustive
enumeration on 250 random instances of up to 7 reads and 7 variants at caps
0 to 3. WhatsHap 2.8's selector differs from its published
procedure~\cite{fischer2016}: after admitting a read it lowers each queued
read's score by the variants the admitted read did not newly cover. Our port
reproduces WhatsHap's selections read for read on 1{,}500 of 1{,}500 small
instances, against 178 of 1{,}500 for the published procedure; the pipelines
use WhatsHap's own compiled selector.

The objective and the accuracy are different quantities: the true haplotypes
can have a higher MEC than a heuristic solution~\cite{duitama2012}, and MEC
can prefer a wrong haplotype at realistic error rates~\cite{majidian2020}. We
therefore report both.

\subsection{Experiments}\label{sec:seldesign}

\paragraph{Data.} HG002 PacBio Sequel II 11\,kb HiFi reads from
GIAB~\cite{zook2016,wagner2022} (27.7 to 38.5-fold per chromosome) and ONT
R10.4.1 super-accuracy reads (\texttt{giab\_2023.05}, about 64-fold), with
seeded subsamples at 20, 15 and 10-fold (HiFi) and 30, 20 and 10-fold (ONT);
HG005 HiFi (about 30-fold) at full depth and 10-fold as a replication; and
the HG002 trio's HiFi reads on chromosomes 16, 18, 21 and 22. Variants are
the biallelic heterozygous SNVs inside the GIAB v4.2.1 benchmark regions on
the 21 autosomes other than chromosome 20, which was the pilot (2{,}015{,}699
for HG002). The truth is GIAB v5.0q~\cite{hansen2026} for HG002 and
transmission from HG006 and HG007 for HG005.

\paragraph{Pipelines and statistics.} Each selection is phased exactly by a
coverage-parameterized DP under the complementary diploid model, at caps 3,
5, 10 and 15. Two phasings are compared on their common pairs (consecutive
variants that the truth and both phasings phase, inside one block of each);
pairs wrong under exactly one phasing are tested with an exact two-sided sign
test, and the difference in errors per 10{,}000 common pairs has a 95 per
cent interval from 2{,}000 bootstrap resamples of 1\,Mb windows. Levels are
Bonferroni-corrected within each hypothesis. A tool-level test runs
\texttt{whatshap phase} 2.8 unchanged except that its read selection is
replaced by exact selection under the same cap, with WhatsHap's own allele
qualities as weights.

\paragraph{Registration.} Each run's plan was a commit fixing chromosomes,
depths, caps, pipelines, truth, statistics, hypotheses and rules, with later
changes as dated amendments. The exceptions to ``plan before data'' are
stated with each result: the HiFi re-score against v5.0q and an
uncalled-variant correction came after the HiFi results were seen (our
library first reported an arbitrary allele at variants inside a selected
read's span that no selected read called, differentially: 146 such variants
inside blocks for WhatsHap's selection and 12 for exact selection at cap 3;
every library run was repeated after the fix), and the vote model below was
built after the HiFi results.

\subsection{Results}\label{sec:selresults}

\paragraph{The selector's distance from the optimum.} At WhatsHap 2.8's
default cap of 15, WhatsHap's selection reaches 0.9327 of the optimum on
HG002 HiFi (worst chromosome 0.9306), 0.9634 on HG002 ONT and 0.9343 on
HG005 HiFi; 0.9025 at cap 3 on HiFi. A random selection under the cap reaches
0.9054 on HG002 HiFi (exploratory).

\paragraph{Where selection changes accuracy.} In our library, selection
changes switch error at cap 3 on HiFi and at caps 3 and 5 on ONT, and at no
larger cap (Table~\ref{tab:regime}). On HiFi at full depth and cap 3, 141
common pairs are wrong only under WhatsHap's selection and 47 only under
exact selection ($p=4.2\times10^{-12}$). On ONT at full depth the counts are
2{,}389 and 666 at cap 3 ($p=5.7\times10^{-226}$) and 351 and 189 at cap 5
($p=2.95\times10^{-12}$); the difference grows as depth falls. HG005
replicates the HiFi pattern: at cap 3, 77 against 20 discordant pairs at full
depth ($p=4.6\times10^{-9}$), and neither cap-5 test is significant. Exact
selection has the shorter block N50 in 7 of the 8 ONT cells at caps 3 and 5
(338 against 658\,kb at cap 3 and full depth).

\paragraph{Tied sites (post hoc).} Inside a phased block, orient each
selected read to the haplotype its calls agree with by weight; a site is
\emph{tied} when the selected reads, so oriented, weigh equally for both
alleles. Flipping a tied site leaves the wMEC cost unchanged, so the reads do
not determine it: our library's DP reports a guessed allele there, and
\texttt{whatshap phase} leaves it unphased. A worse selection leaves more of
them: at cap 3 and full depth, 1{,}640 for WhatsHap's selection against 33 for
exact selection on ONT, 128 against 4 on HG002 HiFi and 105 against 3 on
HG005. With tied sites left unphased in both selections, the ONT cap-3
difference falls from $+8.81$ to $+1.33$ [$+0.80$, $+1.85$] per 10{,}000 at
full depth and from $+15.01$ to $+3.52$ [$+2.92$, $+4.09$] at 10-fold (77 to
85 per cent of it goes) and stays significant at every depth; on HiFi the
cap-3 difference falls by 51 to 86 per cent. This re-score was found after
all registered library results had been scored and decides no hypothesis.

\texttt{whatshap phase} and 485 only with exact selection
($p=8.44\times10^{-28}$, level $0.05/3$), a difference of $+2.06$ [$+1.57$,
$+2.56$] per 10{,}000; on each run's own pairs the switch error falls from

\paragraph{A vote model and its registered predictions.} At the boundary
between adjacent variants, let $i$ be the number of selected reads that call
both. If each such read votes for the wrong relation independently with
probability $q=2e(1-e)$, for a per-allele error $e$, and ties are broken by a
fair coin, the boundary is wrong with probability
\[
f(i)=\Pr[\Bin(i,q)>i/2]+\tfrac12\Pr[\Bin(i,q)=i/2],
\]
and $f(2k)=f(2k-1)$ for every $k\ge1$ (Theorem~1 of Berend and
Sapir~\cite{berend2005}; we claim no novelty). The predicted difference
between selections is the mean of $f(i^{\mathrm{W}})-f(i^{\mathrm{opt}})$
over boundaries, from $e$ measured on chromosome 20 ($0.00503$ on ONT,
$0.00011$ on HG005). Registered rules: R1, at caps 3 and 5 the prediction
lies in the observed interval or within a factor of 2; R2, the model and the
data agree on whether selection matters (prediction at least 0.5 per
10{,}000; interval above 0); R3, the model beats three baselines. On ONT, R1
and R3 held (mean absolute error 0.611 per 10{,}000 against 2.223 to 3.594
for the baselines) and R2 failed in 1 of 16 cells, at cap 5 and full depth,
where the prediction was 0.4959 and the interval excluded 0. On HG005, R1
held in all four cells and R2 failed at both cap-3 cells (predictions 0.4535
and 0.4565). The model is not supported under its registered rules. All
three R2 failures are predictions within 0.05 below the fixed line of 0.5.
The model breaks ties as the library does, so it described the library's
differences with their guesses included.

\paragraph{Other registered tests.} In WhatsHap's pedigree mode, which splits
the cap across a trio, no registered allocation test was supported; every
run had 2 to 8 switch errors in about 164{,}200 pairs of the child, and a
two-sided sign test needs at least 6 discordant pairs to reach 0.05, so two
of the tests could not have succeeded. Two-level orientation of phased
blocks raised HiFi block N50 at cap 3 from 112 to 234\,kb (ratios 1.66 to
2.09 over four depths), as did connectivity-aware selection (233\,kb); the
added pairs carry the extra errors (4.1 to 5.8 per 10{,}000 at 10-fold
against 0.52 to 0.76). Changes to the selection objective or weights gave no
consistent gain: an adaptive reselection loop was worse than exact selection
at cap 3 (349 against 5 discordant pairs), error-optimal selection failed its
HiFi pilot (96 against 39) and neither replicated nor reversed on ONT, and a
posterior correction frozen before its ONT test was not calibrated at any
depth (2.36 times the predicted wrong calls at full depth).

\paragraph{Reading.} At WhatsHap's default cap of 15, and at cap 5, which is
its per-member cap in pedigree mode, no difference was detected inside the
tool on either platform, although the selector reaches only 0.93 to 0.96 of
the optimum. At cap 3 on ONT, exact selection is more accurate and less
contiguous. Most of the larger differences in our library were guessing at
tied sites, so a comparison of selectors should report abstentions beside
errors.
